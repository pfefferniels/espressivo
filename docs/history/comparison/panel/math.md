# W1 Adversarial Panel — Mathematical Rigor Lens

Reviewer: mathematical rigor. Target: `comparison/DESIGN.md` (W1 DRAFT), against
`SURVEY.md` and `survey-algo.md`.

Method: every quantitative claim was treated as guilty until proved. Claims that
survived are listed in §Verified at the end so the conductor knows what was
attacked and held. Numerical work was done in a scratchpad script (Gauss–Legendre
nodes generated by Newton on Legendre polynomials, references by composite GL-30
on 2·10³–2·10⁴ panels); the numbers quoted below are reproducible from the
formulas given.

Severity key: **CAPITAL** = unsound as written, must change. **MAJOR** = claim
overstated/unproven, or a definition ambiguous enough to cause a wrong
implementation. **MINOR** = imprecision worth fixing.

## M1 — CAPITAL. Pair-dependent integration domains destroy the triangle inequality (four instances)

**Claim under attack.** R3: "The *defined* metrics are true (pseudo-)metrics:
per-dimension `d_k` and any fixed-weight aggregate satisfy identity, symmetry,
triangle inequality on the space of documents (modulo semantic equivalence).
Pair-dependent normalization is forbidden and unimplemented."

The design forbids pair-dependent *weights* but then makes the *domain of
integration* pair-dependent in four separate places:

- **(a) R8 / §5.0** — "a span governed by a style name that `styleScope` reports
  `unresolvable` on EITHER side is excluded from that dimension's density on BOTH
  sides (symmetric)".
- **(b) §5.0 / A-Q6** — "unmatched parts are structural findings, excluded from
  all densities (never folded in)".
- **(c) §5.5** — for `absoluteDuration`/`absoluteVelocity`/`detune*`:
  "present-vs-absent is a structural finding" (i.e. contributes 0 to the density).
- **(d) §5.9** — "`milliseconds.timingBasis` mismatch between the sides:
  structural finding, span excluded".

**The general argument.** Write `d(A,B) = ∫_{Ω \ E(A,B)} p^{A,B}`. Instances
(a)–(c) have `E(A,B) = U_A ∪ U_B` for per-document sets `U_X`; (d) is worse,
`E` is not even of that form. In all four the triangle inequality fails, and it
fails maximally: the *zero set* of `d` stops being transitive, so `d` is not a
pseudo-metric at all and the phrase "modulo semantic equivalence" has no
referent — a pseudo-metric's zero set is forced to be an equivalence relation by
the very inequality being claimed.

**Counterexample (a).** Three documents on one timeline, window `[0, L]`,
tempo dimension.
- `B`: a single `<tempo date="0" bpm="cantabile"/>` whose style def does not
  define `cantabile` ⇒ `styleScope` returns `unresolvable` ⇒ `U_B = [0,L]`.
- `A`: constant 60 qbpm. `C`: constant 120 qbpm. `U_A = U_C = ∅`.

Then `d(A,B) = 0` and `d(B,C) = 0` (everything excluded), while
`d(A,C) = L·ln2 / jnd_tempo > 0`. So `d(A,C) ≤ d(A,B) + d(B,C)` reads
`L·ln2/jnd ≤ 0`. Violated by the whole distance. `A ~ B` and `B ~ C` but
`A ≁ C`.

**Counterexample (b).** `A` has part 1 only; `B` and `C` have parts 1 and 2.
Part 1 is byte-identical in all three; parts 2 of `B` and `C` differ arbitrarily.
Then `d(A,B) = d(A,C) = 0` (part 2 unmatched, excluded) and `d(B,C) > 0`.
Same violation. This one is a direct internal contradiction: A-B4/R6 refuse to
drop an absent *map* precisely because "a pair-dependent dimension set would break
R3", and then §5.0 drops an absent *part*, which breaks R3 by the identical
mechanism.

**Counterexample (c).** Articulation `absoluteDuration`: `A` sets 2, `B` omits it,
`C` sets 100. `d(A,B) = d(B,C) = 0` (present-vs-absent is a structural finding),
`d(A,C) = 98/jnd > 0`.

**Repair (one mechanism covers a, c, d).** Do not remove mass from the domain;
give "no comparable value" a *value*. Extend each row's value space by a
distinguished symbol `⊥` and cap the local metric:

    d_row(x,y) = min(|T(x) − T(y)|, 2δ_row),   d_row(x,⊥) = δ_row,   d_row(⊥,⊥) = 0

This is a genuine metric on the extended space (the truncation `min(·, 2δ)`
preserves the axioms; the added point satisfies `d(x,y) ≤ 2δ = d(x,⊥)+d(⊥,y)`
and `d(x,⊥) ≤ d(x,y)+d(y,⊥)` because `δ ≤ d(x,y)+δ`). `δ_row` joins `jnd` and
`κ` as a documented [convention] constant — in JND units a defensible default is
`δ = 10` JND ("an incomparable value counts as ten JNDs, and no single instant
counts as more than twenty"). Every stated goal survives: the density stays
non-negative and defined on all of `Ω`, so the exact decomposition (R4) is
untouched, determinism is untouched, and R3 becomes true rather than aspirational.
As a bonus this same cap resolves M11.

**Repair (b).** Apply R6 to parts as it is already applied to maps: an unmatched
part is compared against the *neutral* curve, not excluded. Report the asymmetry
as a structural note exactly as A-B4 does. This also forces the design to state
the per-part aggregation rule it currently omits entirely (A-Q6 promises
"aggregation to document level by documented rule"; DESIGN never gives one) — and
note that the obvious rule, *mean over matched parts*, is the M1 disease again
(a pair-dependent index set in the denominator). Only a **sum over the union of
parts**, with absent parts neutral, is metric-safe and additive enough for the
closing table.

**If the panel prefers exclusion anyway**, the only sound version is to hoist the
excluded domain to the same tier as the window and the weights: one excluded set
`Ω \ ⋃_i U_i` for the whole corpus, stamped in the report, with `compareMpm`
documented as computing a domain-restricted quantity that is *not* comparable
across pairs. That is strictly worse for the pairwise product, which is the
headline product.

## M2 — CAPITAL. The pair-derived window makes `compareMpm` non-metric

**Claim under attack.** §5.0: "The comparison window is `[0, end]` with `end` =
the MSM score end when an MSM is supplied, else **the max over both documents of
the last dated instruction** […] One window per pair". R3 then claims metricity
unconditionally, and §1.1 reports `d_k / L` as the piece-length mean.

**Counterexample** (no MSM, no invariance mode, nothing exotic):
- `A`: one `<tempo date="0" bpm="60"/>`. Last dated instruction: 0.
- `B`: `<tempo date="0" bpm="60"/>`, `<tempo date="100" bpm="120"/>`.
- `C`: `<tempo date="0" bpm="60"/>`, `<tempo date="200" bpm="60"/>` — a legal
  no-op restatement (the design elevates exactly this case to a principle in
  A-B2).

Windows: `W(A,B) = [0,100]`, `W(A,C) = [0,200]`, `W(B,C) = [0,200]`.
`d(A,B) = 0` (on `[0,100]` the curves agree a.e.; the divergence at `t = 100` is
measure zero under the right-continuous reading). `d(A,C) = 0` (identical
curves). `d(B,C) = 100·ln2/jnd > 0`. Triangle: `100·ln2/jnd ≤ 0 + 0`. Violated.

The design is *aware* of the mechanism — §5.0 and §8 say "one window per CORPUS
(§8), so matrix entries are values of one function" — but applies the fix only at
corpus level while R3 makes an unconditional claim about "the space of
documents", and the primary product `compareMpm` is pairwise. §7.4's per-document
centering inherits the same defect and adds nothing new: `h ↦ h − mean_W(h)` is a
fine per-document canonicalization *for a fixed W*, so 'level' is metric-safe
exactly where the window is; it is not metric-safe where the window moves.

**Repair.** The window must be a function of the *piece*, never of the pair.
1. State that the metric guarantees hold only for a **piece-derived window**:
   MSM score end, or an explicit `options.window`, or a corpus-shared window.
2. Make the pair-derived fallback explicitly non-metric: report
   `windowRule: 'pair-derived'` together with a `metricGuarantee:
   'unconditional' | 'window-restricted'` flag, and say in the docs that
   `compareMpm` numbers computed under `'pair-derived'` are not comparable across
   pairs (and must not be assembled into a matrix by a caller).
3. Note in §8 that the corpus window is *corpus*-dependent (adding an item moves
   `end` and therefore every entry) — the same reproducibility caveat already
   carried, correctly, for `normalization: 'corpus'`.
4. `d_k / L` is a pair-dependent rescaling of a pair-dependent quantity; under
   a piece-derived window it is fine, otherwise it compounds the problem. Say so.

## M3 — CAPITAL. §6.2's pricing rule violates R5's `scriptCost ≥ d_curve`; `reworking` goes negative

**Claim under attack.** R5: "`scriptCost ≥ d_curve` per dimension, slack surfaced
as `reworking`"; §6.2: "An op's cost is **the change it causes in the dimension's
density**… inserting a renderer-skipped or no-op instruction costs exactly 0 and
is marked `free`"; §6.4: "The DP prices ops against the original A-context (upper
bound on the true shortest-path cost)". survey-algo §2.A.6's caveat (i) asserts
the same.

**The parenthetical is false, and R5 falls with it.** Pricing against the
*original* context is not an upper bound on the path cost — it can be a strict
*under*-estimate, because an op that is a no-op in A's context may do real work
once earlier ops have been applied.

**Counterexample.** Tempo dimension, window `[0,10]` quarters.
- `A`: `I@0 bpm=60`, `J@5 bpm=60` (a no-op restatement — legal, and the case A-B2
  singles out).
- `B`: `I@0 bpm=120`, no `J`.

`d_curve = ∫₀¹⁰ |ln120 − ln60| = 10·ln2` (÷ jnd throughout).
The DP's cheapest script is {substitute `I`: 60→120, delete `J`}:
- substitute `I`, priced in A's context: in `A`, `I` governs `[0,5)` because `J`
  follows, so the change is `5·ln2`;
- delete `J`, priced in A's context: removing `J` from `A` leaves the curve
  identically 60 — **cost exactly 0, marked `free` by §6.2's own rule**.

`scriptCost = 5·ln2 = d_curve / 2`. R5's inequality fails by a factor of two and
`reworking = scriptCost − d_curve = −5·ln2 < 0`. This is not a corner: the DP
*minimises* cost, so it will actively prefer this script, and the construction
generalises — any deletion whose effect is masked by a later-superseded
instruction is priced free.

Proposition 3 (survey-algo §2.A.6) is itself correct: the *shortest-path* metric
`d_edit` does dominate `d_curve`. The defect is that the DP's number is not
`d_edit` and is not above it.

**Repair.** Price ops sequentially, not against A.
1. Define `scriptCost = Σ_i ‖Φ(M_i) − Φ(M_{i−1})‖₁` over the script applied in
   date order, `M_0 = A`, `M_n = B`. This telescopes correctly and satisfies
   `scriptCost ≥ ‖Φ(A) − Φ(B)‖₁ = d_curve` by the L¹ triangle inequality — the
   guarantee then holds *by construction* rather than by hope, and `reworking ≥ 0`
   is a theorem. In the counterexample the sequential price is `5·ln2 + 5·ln2 =
   10·ln2 = d_curve`, `reworking = 0`, which is also the musically right answer.
   The state the DP needs for this (the prevailing value at the op's date) is
   determined by the DP cell, so this is implementable without leaving `O(nm·q)`.
2. `free` must be redefined as "zero sequential cost", not "zero cost against A",
   or A-B2's guarantee ("a no-op encoding difference costs 0 by pricing") is
   simultaneously too generous (M3) and unrelated to what the renderer performs.
3. §6.3 is separately confused: "`replayedDelta` (achieved distance to B)" is, if
   the script is complete, *identically* `d_curve` — a recomputation, not a third
   quantity, so "the triple […] all three exact" advertises two numbers as three.
   Make `replayedDelta` the sequential cost of (1), which is the quantity that
   actually verifies the script and bounds `d_curve` from above.

## M4 — CAPITAL. Proposition 1 is false for every dimension containing a transition; P-C5 cannot pass

**Claim under attack.** §1.3: "`exaggerateMpm` acts on T-space curves as
`g ↦ c + s(g − c)`. Therefore, for every dimension the two modules share:
exaggeration by `s` leaves `d_shape` at 0, multiplies gain by |s|, moves level
affinely"; P-C5 pins `shape ≈ 0 (≤ ε)`, "gain scaled by |s|", "distance monotone
in |ln s|".

**The error.** `exaggerateMpm` acts on *attribute values* — the curve's
breakpoints — as `x ↦ T⁻¹(s·T(x))`. The renderer then **interpolates between the
exaggerated breakpoints in the raw space, not in T-space**: tempo interpolates in
bpm (`bpm₀ + (bpm₁−bpm₀)u^e`, §5.1), dynamics interpolates in volume
(smoothstep, §5.3), rubato's displacement is a power law in `τ/frameLength`
(§5.2). Exponentiation does not commute with linear interpolation, so the
exaggerated *curve* is not the affine image of the original curve. Proposition 1
holds exactly only for **piecewise-constant** dimensions.

**Numbers.** Tempo transition 60 → 120 over one cell, `e = 1`, exaggerated with
`s = 2` about `μ = 60`. At `u = 0.5` the renderer produces **150 qbpm**; the
affine-image-in-T-space claim predicts **135 qbpm**. Evaluating the §1.2
decomposition of the two curves on `([0,1], du)`:

| quantity | value | claim |
|---|---|---|
| `r` | 0.996340288960 | 1 |
| `d_shape = √(2(1−r))` | **8.56·10⁻²** | `≤ ε ≈ 10⁻¹²` |
| `σ_B / σ_A` | **1.9316** | exactly 2 |
| `ℓ_B` | 4.942737 | `c + s(ℓ_A − c) = 4.866933` |

All three legs fail, by 10–11 orders of magnitude relative to the pinned `ε`.
P-C5 as written fails on any fixture containing a tempo or dynamics transition —
i.e. on the real corpora, which survey-code measured as transition-dominated
(`transition.to` in 79–100% of files).

**Two further defects in the same claim.**
- *The factors vector matters and is unspecified.* The comparison `tempo`
  dimension is `⊇ {tempo, tempoShape}` (§3). `tempoShape` (`meanTempoAt`) moves
  the exponent `e`, which changes the curve's shape even for constant-endpoint
  spans. Unless the test's factors vector pins `tempoShape = 1` (and
  `dynamicsShape = 1`, `ornamentSpacing = 1`, …), the shape term is nonzero for a
  second, independent reason.
- *"Monotone in |ln s|" is wrong even where the law holds.* For a
  piecewise-constant curve, `d(mpm, exagg_s(mpm)) = |1 − s|·∫|g − c|` — monotone
  in `|1 − s|`, not in `|ln s|`. The two orders disagree across `s = 1`:
  `s = 0.1` gives `|ln s| = 2.30` with `d = 0.9·∫|g−c|`, while `s = 3` gives
  `|ln s| = 1.10` with `d = 2·∫|g−c|`. A test sampling `s` on both sides of 1
  fails. Also `s < 0` gives `r = −1`, hence `d_shape = 2`, not 0.

**Repair.** State the true proposition and test the true proposition.
> **Proposition 1 (corrected).** `exaggerateMpm(s)` acts on each registry row's
> value as multiplication by `s` in that row's T-space. Consequently, for a
> dimension whose comparison curve is `T⁻¹` applied pointwise to a
> **piecewise-constant** family of row values all transformed with the same `s`,
> the T-space curve satisfies `g ↦ c + s(g − c)`, whence `shape` is invariant,
> `gain ↦ |s|·gain`, `level ↦ c + s(level − c)`. For dimensions whose renderer
> interpolates in a space other than T (tempo, dynamics, rubato, pedal), the law
> holds **at the breakpoints only**; the interpolated interior is not the affine
> image and `d_shape > 0` in general.

P-C5 then splits into: (i) an exact law on constant-only fixtures for all shared
dimensions, with `factors` pinning every shape knob to 1, `s > 0`, and
monotonicity in `|1 − s|`; (ii) a breakpoint-level law tested on the *sampled
breakpoint values* for transition-bearing dimensions; (iii) a documented,
*measured* bound on `d_shape` for transitions, pinned as a regression anchor
rather than asserted to be zero. This is still strong cross-module evidence — it
is just true.

## M5 — CAPITAL. Exact-date pre-pinning before the alignment DP breaks the triangle inequality for event dimensions

**Claim under attack.** §5.6: "Matching: **by exact date, then by the alignment
DP (§6)** — the event dimensions and the edit path share one aligner, so the
semantic distance and the script are consistent by construction." §5.5 uses the
same fallback chain for articulation atoms (A-B5).

**Background.** The unpinned event distance — minimise
`Σ_matched (Σ_rows|ΔT|/jnd + λ|Δdate|) + Σ_unmatched (deviation from neutral)`
over monotone alignments — *is* a pseudo-metric: substitution cost is a metric on
the event alphabet, `gap(a) ≤ sub(a,b) + gap(b)` is the componentwise triangle
inequality in ℝ, and the DP's `min` lets any script compose. Pre-pinning destroys
this, because "match all events sharing a date" is a constraint that is not
closed under composition of alignments.

**Counterexample.** One attribute per event, neutral 0, `gap(e) = |v_e|`,
`sub(e,f) = |v_e − v_f| + λ|Δdate|`. Fix any `λ > 0`, then choose `Δ` with
`λΔ < v`:
- `A = { a₁@0 : v, a₂@Δ : v }`
- `B = { b₁@Δ/2 : v, b₂@3Δ/2 : v }`
- `C = { c₁@Δ : v, c₂@2Δ : v }`

`d(A,B)`: no shared dates, so pure DP — match `a₁↔b₁`, `a₂↔b₂`, cost `λΔ`
(vs. `4v` for all-indel). Likewise `d(B,C) = λΔ`.
`d(A,C)`: dates `Δ` are shared, so the pin forces `a₂↔c₁` (cost 0) and leaves
`a₁`, `c₂` as indels: `d(A,C) = 2v`. (Unpinned it would be `2λΔ`.)
Triangle: `2v ≤ 2λΔ` is false by choice of `Δ`. Since `Δ` is free, the violation
survives *any* value of the `[convention]` constant `λ_date`.

**A second, independent defect in the same sentence.** The semantic density and
the DP objective are asserted to be "consistent by construction", but §5.6
defines the matched-event contribution as the row-wise T-space delta with **no
date term**, while §6.2 prices event ops with "a date-shift term
`λ_date·|Δdate|`". If the two functionals differ, the reported semantic distance
is a *non-minimal* functional evaluated at the argmin of a *different* one, which
has no metric argument at all — and it prices a matched ornament displaced by
half a bar at zero.

**Repair.**
1. Delete the exact-date pre-pass. Let the DP do all matching; an exact-date
   match is already free of date cost, so the DP selects it whenever it is
   optimal, and where it is not optimal the pin was wrong anyway.
2. State one functional. The semantic event distance **is** the DP objective,
   date term included; §5.6 must carry `λ_date` explicitly.
3. Keep the `xml:id` / `noteid.ref` pin (survey-algo §2.A.5) as an *opportunistic*
   equality of identity, and note that id-matching is transitive when all three
   documents carry ids, so it composes and is metric-safe — unlike date pinning,
   which is transitive only when all three documents happen to share the date.

## M6 — MAJOR. The `ε ≈ 1e−12` accuracy claim is false at both ends of the legal `meanTempoAt` range

**Claim under attack.** R3: "target relative ε ≈ 1e−12, stated in the report";
§5.0: "fixed-order Gauss–Legendre (order 10), **subdividing tempo cells with
exponent `e < 1`** (unbounded derivative at cell start)".

`e = ln0.5 / ln(meanTempoAt)` with `meanTempoAt ∈ (0,1)` legal throughout. As
`mTA → 1⁻`, `e → +∞`; as `mTA → 0⁺`, `e → 0⁺`. The design subdivides only the
second. Measured relative error of GL-10 on one cell of `ln(60 + 60·u^e)`:

| `meanTempoAt` | `e` | GL-10 rel. err | design's rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.50 | 1 | 1.2·10⁻¹⁵ | (fine) |
| 0.707 | 2 | 3.7·10⁻¹⁵ | (fine) |
| **0.933** | **10** | **7.0·10⁻⁹** | not subdivided |
| **0.990** | **69** | **4.4·10⁻⁵** | not subdivided |
| **0.995** | **150** | **2.7·10⁻⁴** | not subdivided |
| 0.25 | 0.5 | 1.9·10⁻⁵ | subdivided → 4.8·10⁻⁹ ¹ |
| 0.031 | 0.2 | 5.1·10⁻⁵ | subdivided → 7.6·10⁻⁸ ¹ |

¹ with eight geometric panels toward 0 — the design specifies *that* cells are
subdivided but never *how*, so this is a charitable reading; it still misses
`1e−12` by four orders of magnitude.

`meanTempoAt = 0.93` is an entirely ordinary musical value (a late-weighted
ritardando), and it already costs nine digits.

Two further contributors to the same claim:
- §5.9 specifies `Φ⁻¹` "via Cody/Acklam rational approximations". Acklam's
  inverse-normal is `|err| < 1.15·10⁻⁹`; survey-algo §5 offered "optionally
  refined by one Halley step to ~1e−15" and DESIGN dropped the refinement. Any
  W₂ number touching a truncated normal is therefore ~1e−9 accurate.
- §5.1's `cumulativeDrift` is computed "with the renderer's own Simpson
  integration" at one sub-interval per sixteenth — an entirely different (and much
  larger) error, reported next to a single `epsilon` field (§9).

**Repair.**
1. Subdivide on **both** sides: `e < 1` (endpoint singularity at `u = 0`) and
   `e > e_max` (boundary layer at `u = 1`). Better, eliminate the problem
   analytically: substitute `u = z^{1/e}`, `du = (1/e)z^{1/e−1}dz`, which turns
   `u^e` into `z` and makes the integrand smooth for every `e`; then GL-10 is
   back at machine precision for the whole legal range. Specify the rule and the
   panel count — as written, two implementers will produce different numbers.
2. Add the Halley refinement to `Φ⁻¹`, or report the W₂ table's `ε` separately.
3. Replace the single `epsilon` field with a per-dimension (or at least
   per-family) `epsilon` record; a single global constant cannot be true of
   step dimensions (exact), tempo (quadrature), imprecision (special functions)
   and `cumulativeDrift` (Simpson) simultaneously.

## M7 — MAJOR. Sign-change bisection misses interior double crossings; the resulting error is 1.5·10⁻²

**Claim under attack.** §5.0: "locating sign changes of `g_A − g_B` by bisection
(fixed 50 iterations) **so `|·|` never crosses a kink inside a quadrature cell**".

Bisection can only find a crossing it is given a bracket for, i.e. one detected by
opposite signs at the cell endpoints. `g_A − g_B` may cross **twice** inside a
cell with equal signs at both ends. For two tempo transitions on the same span,
`g_A − g_B = 0 ⟺ Δ_a u^{p} − Δ_b u^{q} = b₀ − a₀`, a generalized polynomial with
two sign changes in its coefficient sequence, hence up to two positive roots
(Descartes' rule extends to real exponents).

**Counterexample, both sides legal MPM.**
`A`: `bpm₀ = 72.6`, `bpm₁ = 132.6`, `meanTempoAt = 0.7071` (⇒ `e = 2`).
`B`: `bpm₀ = 60`, `bpm₁ = 120`, `meanTempoAt = 0.5` (⇒ `e = 1`).
Then `g_A − g_B` is `+1.91·10⁻¹` at `u = 0`, `−2.70·10⁻²` at `u = 0.5`,
`+9.99·10⁻²` at `u = 1`; the exact crossings are `u = 0.3` and `u = 0.7`. The
design's mechanism sees equal endpoint signs, finds no bracket, and integrates
`|g_A − g_B|` with one GL-10 cell:

    GL-10 (as designed)      0.043662394130
    reference (split at 0.3, 0.7)  0.044319719025
    relative error                 1.48·10⁻²

That is **ten orders of magnitude** past the advertised `ε`, and it is silent —
nothing in the pipeline reports it.

**Repair.** Bracket by structure, not by endpoint signs. For power-vs-power tempo
cells the difference `f(u) = a₀ + Δ_a u^p − b₀ − Δ_b u^q` has
`f'(u) = pΔ_a u^{p−1} − qΔ_b u^{q−1} = 0` at the single interior critical point
`u* = (qΔ_b / pΔ_a)^{1/(p−q)}` (for `p ≠ q`), so splitting the cell at `u*` gives
two monotone branches and the existing bisection is then complete and correct.
For Bézier-vs-Bézier dynamics, split at the cells' own control-point-derived
extrema and, failing a closed form, state the subdivision depth and treat the
kink-detection as heuristic in the docs rather than claiming `|·|` "never" crosses
a kink inside a cell. Whatever is chosen, the ε claim must be conditioned on it.

## M8 — MAJOR. The level/gain/shape lemma needs a probability measure; §5.0's `w ≡ 1` is not one, and the σ = 0 conventions are unstated

**Claim under attack.** §1.2: "for curves in T-space over `(time, w·dt)` and
equally for quantile functions over `([0,1], du)`,
`‖h_A − h_B‖₂² = (ℓ_A−ℓ_B)² + (σ_A−σ_B)² + 2σ_Aσ_B(1−r)`". §5.0: "`w(t) = 1`
(score ticks) by default".

survey-algo §2.B states the lemma with "`μ` a probability measure" and
"`w(t) ≥ 0`, **`∫w = 1`**". DESIGN dropped the normalization while keeping the
identity verbatim. With `w ≡ 1` on a window of length `L`, `(time, w·dt)` has
total mass `L`, and the identity becomes

    ‖h_A − h_B‖₂² = L·(ℓ_A − ℓ_B)² + (σ_A − σ_B)² + 2σ_Aσ_B(1 − r)

if `ℓ` is the *mean*, and breaks outright if an implementer reads `ℓ_X = ∫h_X dμ`
literally as an unnormalized integral. Meanwhile the *headline* density
deliberately uses the unnormalized measure (that is what makes `d_k` a mass in
JND·quarters and what makes the attribution table close). So the design needs two
measures and names only one. This is precisely the "ambiguous enough to cause a
wrong implementation" bar: `d_level` is either in nepers or in neper·√quarters
depending on which reading an implementer picks.

**Degenerate cases, also unstated.** §1.2 says only "`r` on a constant window is
`null` and the window marked shapeless — never 0". It does not say what happens to
the *identity*. For `σ_A = 0 < σ_B`: the identity is still exact, because the
shape *term* `2σ_Aσ_B(1−r)` is 0 whatever `r` would have been, leaving
`‖h_A−h_B‖² = (ℓ_A−ℓ_B)² + σ_B²` — which is correct, since `h_A` is constant.
But an implementation that stores `shape = null` and then sums the three terms
gets `NaN`, and §9's result shape has a single `shape` field that cannot express
"the shape *distance* is null while the shape *term* is 0".

**Repair.**
1. State explicitly: the decomposition is computed on the **normalized** measure
   `dμ = w dt / ∫w dt` (and `∫w` must be recomputed per window and per invariance
   mode); the headline density is computed on the unnormalized `w dt`. Give both
   symbols distinct names in the spec.
2. Report four fields, not three ambiguous ones: `level = |ℓ_A−ℓ_B|`,
   `gain = |σ_A−σ_B|`, `shape = √(2(1−r))` **or `null`**, `r` or `null`, plus the
   closing check `‖h_A−h_B‖₂²` with the convention "shape term := 0 when
   `σ_Aσ_B = 0`" written down. State that `level`/`gain`/`shape` are the
   square-*roots* while the identity is in squares — §1.2 currently mixes them in
   one paragraph.

## M9 — MAJOR. The segment/table specification does not determine a unique table

**Claims under attack.** §1.1: "ranked deviation segments: maximal-scoring
segments of the thresholded density"; §7.3: "Cell scores `w_c·(p_k(c) − τ)` feed
Ruzzo–Tompa; its maximal segments are canonical (no tie rules needed), ranked by
integral mass. The attribution table rows = dimensions, columns = ranked segments
+ one below-threshold remainder column".

Five distinct defects:

**(a) Whose density defines the columns?** The score formula is indexed by `k`
(per dimension), so Ruzzo–Tompa yields **eleven different segment sets**. But the
table needs **one** column set shared by all rows, and `ComparisonResult.report`
carries a single `segments` list. As written the table is not defined.
*Repair:* define the columns on the weighted aggregate density
`p_D(t) = Σ_k ω_k p_k(t)` against `τ_D = Σ_k ω_k τ_k`; per-dimension segment lists
may ship as a secondary, non-closing product.

**(b) The segments are cell-quantized, so §1.1's description is false.** `p_k` is
continuous inside a cell for tempo/dynamics, so `p_k − τ` changes sign at interior
points. The RT segments are maximal-scoring segments of the *cell-aggregated*
profile; their boundaries land on refinement-grid edges, which can be far from the
true `τ`-crossing (a constant-tempo span is one cell and may be many bars).
*Repair:* add the roots of `p_k − τ` to the grid for the segment pass — the
machinery is already specified for `|g_A − g_B|` kinks (and must be fixed per M7);
or restate the claim as "maximal-scoring segments of the cell-aggregated
thresholded density" and say the boundaries are grid-resolution.

**(c) `p_k(c)` is undefined for a cell.** With atoms in the measure (§5.0), a cell
containing an event has no point density. The score must be defined as
`(mass of p_k in c) − τ·w(c)`, not `w_c·(p_k(c) − τ)` with `p_k(c)` a sample —
otherwise every atom either vanishes or blows up, depending on the reading. Note
zero-width cells (co-dated instructions are legal, survey-algo §4) then behave
correctly: score = atom mass, `τ·0 = 0`.

**(d) "canonical, no tie rules needed" is true for the *set*, not for the
*ranking*.** Ruzzo–Tompa's maximality definition does survive exact zeros — a
segment extended by a zero-score cell contains a proper subsequence of equal
score and therefore fails maximality, so boundary zeros are excluded and the set
is unique. (I attacked this and it held.) But the design ranks the segments by
`∫p` — a *different* functional from the `(p−τ)` score that produced them — and
two segments can have equal mass. R2 promises "all ties broken by documented
rules"; this one is not documented. *Repair:* rank by mass, break ties by earliest
start date, then by shortest length.

**(e) With M1's exclusions in place, "segments partition the timeline" (R4) is
false**, and the excluded set is *per dimension* while the columns are shared — so
one column means different things in different rows. If M1 is repaired by the
`⊥`-extension there is nothing to fix; if exclusions survive, the table needs an
explicit `excluded` column per row and R4's wording must change.

**What actually closes (worth stating explicitly, since §7.3 gives a "because"
that proves less than it claims):** closure is *independent* of how segments are
chosen. For **any** partition `{S_s}` of the integration domain,
`Σ_s ∫_{S_s} p_k = d_k` by countable additivity, and `Σ_k ω_k Σ_s c_{k,s} = D`
because `D` is a weighted sum. Ruzzo–Tompa only decides *which* partition is
reported. Saying this plainly protects the headline capability from being
entangled with the thresholding.

## M10 — MAJOR. `invariance` is ill-defined for six of the eleven dimensions, and `'level-gain'` is undefined on constant curves

**Claim under attack.** §7.4: "Per dimension, `invariance: 'none' | 'level' |
'level-gain'`… `'level'`: each document's curve is centered by its own window mean
before differencing… `'level-gain'`: centered and σ-normalized per document".
§9 types the option as `Partial<Record<ComparisonDimension, …>>` — i.e. it is
offered for all eleven.

- **No single curve exists** for `articulation` (multi-attribute: five spaces,
  step profiles plus atoms), `ornamentation` (discrete events), and the three
  `imprecision*` dimensions (distribution-valued). "The window mean" of a
  distribution-valued object, or of a sum of rows in nepers, quarters, ms and
  velocity units, is not defined. That is six of eleven dimensions for which the
  option type-checks and has no semantics.
- **`'level-gain'` divides by σ**, and σ = 0 on a constant curve — which §2.B.6
  calls "*completely ordinary* in this data". The design specifies the `r = null`
  convention for the interpretive table but says nothing here; as written this is
  a division by zero on the most common input in the corpus.
- **`'level-gain'` contradicts P-C5.** Under it, `d(mpm, exaggerateMpm(mpm, s)) =
  0` for every `s > 0` (pure shape comparison), while P-C5 asserts the distance is
  monotone in `s`. The two options must be documented as mutually exclusive.
- The window-mean is the *pair-dependent* window mean — see M2. `'level'` is
  metric-safe exactly when the window is piece-derived, and not otherwise.

**Repair.** (i) Define invariance **per registry row**, not per dimension: each
row's curve is centered by its own window mean, and a dimension's mode applies to
all of its curve-valued rows. (ii) Declare the option unsupported (typed error, or
`null` with a note) for event- and distribution-valued rows, or define it for
distributions as a location shift of the law (which is meaningful and cheap:
subtract the span-weighted mean of the law's mean). (iii) For `σ = 0` under
`'level-gain'`, define the canonical curve as identically 0 and mark the dimension
`shapeless` — that keeps the map per-document and total. (iv) State that
`'level'`/`'level-gain'` require a piece-derived or corpus-shared window for R3 to
hold.

## M11 — MAJOR. `T` is `−∞` on legal input and the survey's clamp guard was dropped from the design

**Claim under attack.** §5.8: `@curvature`/`@protraction` "remain rows for the
edit path's substitution pricing, in the same spaces expression assigns
(boundary-power-low / logit(−1,1))". §9: "every numeric finite or null".

survey-algo §1.3(d) and §4 flag this explicitly: `T(x) = ln(1−x) = −∞` at

at 1, `boundary-power-high` at 0, `logit` at both bounds, `log-around-*` at 0 —
and add the `bpm ≤ 0` typed error to §9's error list.

## M12 — MAJOR. `ω_k = 1` is not the neutral choice the design says it is

**Claim under attack.** §7.2: "default `ω_k = 1` for every dimension — the JND
normalization has already made dimensions commensurable, so unequal default
weights would be a second, hidden opinion."

The JND normalization makes each *row* commensurable. It does not make each
*dimension* commensurable, because a dimension's `d_k` is a **sum over its rows
and over its atoms** (§3: "multi-attribute dimension: rows aggregate in JND
units"; §5.5: "matched atoms contribute `Σ_rows |T(x_A) − T(x_B)|/jnd_row`"). So
`d_articulation` accumulates over seven rows and over every note-anchored atom in
the piece, while `d_tempo` accumulates over one curve. Two documents that differ
by one JND uniformly in every attribute do **not** produce equal `d_k` across
dimensions — they produce `d_k` proportional to each dimension's row count and
event count. `ω_k = 1` therefore *is* an opinion (it up-weights row-rich,
event-rich dimensions), just an undeclared one; the stated justification is
unsound.

**Repair.** Keep `ω_k = 1` if desired — it is defensible and it is metric-safe —
but replace the justification with the truth, and give the reader the lever:
report per dimension both the raw `d_k` and `d_k / (#rows × #atom-sites)` (or the
per-row breakdown, which the registry already affords), so a caller can see what
`ω = 1` is actually weighting. State the row-aggregation rule (sum, not mean, not
max) explicitly in §3 — it currently appears only as an aside in a table cell.

## M13 — MAJOR. W₁ is not "piecewise-polynomial, exact" for the Gaussian family, and the escape hatch is not always an artifact

**Claims under attack.** §5.9: "Gaussian(0, sd) truncated to limits (rejection
with escape hatch — **modeled as the truncated law**; the escape hatch's leakage
is a renderer artifact, noted in docs)… Headline density: `W₁(law_A(t),law_B(t))/
jnd` per span — **piecewise-polynomial CDF integration, exact**".

**(a) Not piecewise polynomial.** The truncated normal's CDF is
`(Φ(x/σ) − Φ(lo/σ)) / (Φ(hi/σ) − Φ(lo/σ))`. `Φ` is not a polynomial, so
`∫|F_A − F_B|dx` against any other family requires numerical quadrature *and*
transcendental root-finding to locate the sign changes of `F_A − F_B`. "Exact" is
wrong for one of MPM's four families — and the most commonly authored one.
(Uniform: piecewise linear ✓. Triangular, clipped: piecewise quadratic with jump
discontinuities at the clips — the atoms do not break polynomiality, only
continuity, and the crossings are roots of a quadratic, so that part of the claim
holds. `distribution.list`: piecewise constant ✓.)

**(b) The escape hatch is the whole law in a reachable case.** The renderer
rejects for 10 000 attempts and then emits an out-of-limits value
(`RandomNumberProvider.ts:306-352`). With `q = P(outside limits)`, the modelled
truncated law is right only up to `q^10000`. That is negligible for ordinary
limits (`q^10000 = 0.5` needs `q = 1 − 6.9·10⁻⁵`), but it is **not** negligible in
the degenerate case `limit.lower == limit.upper` — a natural way to author "no
imprecision here" — where `q = 1` exactly: the renderer then always emits an
unconditioned `N(0, σ)` draw, while the design's model is a point mass. The
comparison would report distance 0 between a document that performs full Gaussian
noise and one that performs none. Worse, the truncated law's normalizer
`Φ(hi/σ) − Φ(lo/σ)` is 0 there, so the quantile formula in §5.9 is `0/0`.

**Repair.** Model the renderer's actual law as the mixture it is:

    L = (1 − q^N)·TruncNormal(0, σ; lo, hi) + q^N·N(0, σ),   N = 10000

Its CDF is a convex combination of two available CDFs, so W₁ costs nothing extra;
it degenerates correctly to `N(0,σ)` at `q = 1` and to the truncated law for any
ordinary limit window; and it removes the `0/0`. Then restate the accuracy claim
per family: "exact (closed-form piecewise-polynomial CDFs) for
uniform/triangular/clipped/list; quadrature at `ε_Φ` for any span involving a
Gaussian".

## M14 — MAJOR. The imprecision density is specified two incompatible ways across the two binding documents

**Claim under attack.** DESIGN §5.9: "Headline density:
`W₁(law_A(t), law_B(t)) / jnd` **per span**". survey-algo §3 (L2) and §2.D:
"imprecision → **W₂ per span / span width**", "imprecision spans give W₂
**divided over the span they govern**".

These differ by a factor of the span length, i.e. by orders of magnitude, and they
differ in kind:
- DESIGN's reading — density `= W₁(law_A(t), law_B(t))/jnd` as a pointwise
  function of `t` on the common refinement — is **correct and metric-safe**:
  `W₁` is a metric on laws, so `∫ W₁(μ_t, ν_t) dt` satisfies the triangle
  inequality pointwise and then by integration, and the units come out as
  JND·quarters like every other dimension.
- survey-algo's reading — divide by "the span they govern" — is **not**: A's and
  B's imprecision instructions have *different* spans, so "the span" is
  pair-dependent, the resulting quantity is not an integral of a fixed density,
  and it makes a difference lasting one bar equal to the same difference lasting
  the whole piece.

DESIGN is right and the survey is wrong, but the survey is cited as the evidence
base and an implementer reading both will not know which to build.

**Repair.** State the definition unambiguously in §5.9 — "the density at `t` is
`W₁` between the two laws prevailing at `t`, in JND per quarter; `d_k` is its
integral, so a difference contributes in proportion to how long it is performed" —
and add a one-line note that survey-algo's per-span normalization is superseded,
with the reason. Add a fixture pinning the span-length proportionality.

## M15 — MAJOR. P-C3 as specified cannot falsify R3

**Claim under attack.** P-C3: "sampled fixture/corpus triples, per-dimension and
aggregate, `d(A,C) ≤ d(A,B) + d(B,C) + 3ε`."

Every counterexample in M1, M2 and M5 is invisible to this test as written:
- triples drawn from a *corpus* run share one window, so M2's violation cannot
  appear (and triples drawn pairwise will have three different windows, which is
  what makes it appear — the test must say which);
- the violations need fixtures with an **unresolvable style name**, an
  **unmatched part**, a **present-vs-absent replacement attribute**, and a
  **timingBasis mismatch** — none of which is in the W2 fixture list (§11), which
  derives from the official samples;
- M5's violation needs event dates that are shared between two of the three
  documents and not the third.

A triangle test whose fixtures cannot express the known failure modes is a test
that certifies the claim rather than probing it.

**Repair.** Make P-C3 adversarial by construction: a dedicated fixture family
whose members pairwise exercise each M1 instance, plus an explicit assertion that
the three windows are equal (or the test is run under a piece-derived window).
Add **P-C3b: transitivity of the zero set** — `d(A,B) = 0 ∧ d(B,C) = 0 ⟹
d(A,C) = 0` — which is the cheapest possible detector for every M1-class defect
and would have caught all four instances.

## M16 — MAJOR. The mirrored-script promise contradicts the stated traceback precedence

**Claims under attack.** R2: "Symmetry is bit-exact: `compare(a,b)` and
`compare(b,a)` agree to the last bit on every number". §6.4/P-C6: "Traceback
precedence `substitute > delete > insert`, then lowest source index; **a
transposed input yields the mirrored script** (pinned test)."

A fixed preference for `delete` over `insert` is not transposition-covariant.
Transposing the inputs maps "delete `a_i`" to "insert `a_i`", so at a DP cell
where delete and insert tie, the `A→B` run takes the delete branch and the `B→A`
run — facing the mirrored tie — takes *its* delete branch, which is the mirror
image of the `A→B` run's **insert** branch. The two scripts are then not mirrors.
survey-algo §2.H is explicit that these ties are "structural rather than
accidental" (equal-cost `insert-then-delete` vs `delete-then-insert`), so this is
the common case, not a corner.

Note what *does* hold, so the repair is scoped correctly: the semantic path's
bit-exact symmetry survives scrutiny. The sorted-union grid is literally the same
array in both directions; `|x − y|` is bit-exact symmetric; date-ordered Neumaier
summation is order-fixed; `bezier.tForDate` bisects a single document's own curve
and is unaffected by the other; and the sign-change bisection is symmetric
**provided** the bracket update is written as a sign *comparison*
(`sign(f(m)) == sign(f(a))`) rather than `f(m) > 0`, since `f ↦ −f` is exact in
IEEE754 and inverts both tests consistently. That proviso is not currently in the
design and should be, or the promise fails on a detail no reviewer will re-derive.

**Repair.** Adopt survey-algo §4's escape hatch for the *edit* path only: compute
the script once in canonical order `sort([idA, idB])` and invert it (swap
insert↔delete, `dateA`↔`dateB`) for the other direction. Then the mirroring is
true by construction and the precedence rule keeps its determinism role. Add the
sign-comparison proviso to §5.0's bisection sentence.

## M17 — MAJOR. Matched events at different dates have no defined location, which breaks the table's symmetry

**Claim under attack.** §5.0: "`p_k` = an absolutely continuous part … plus
**atoms at event dates**"; §5.6 matches events across *different* dates (that is
the point of the aligner and of the `λ_date` term).

When `a@d_A` matches `b@d_B` with `d_A ≠ d_B`, the pair's cost is a single atom —
but at which date? The design never says. Every available reading has a
consequence:
- at `d_A`: `compare(a,b)` and `compare(b,a)` place the atom in **different
  segments**, so the attribution table's columns differ between the two
  directions. R2's "agree to the last bit on every number" and P-C2's
  "bit-identical serialized reports (modulo the a/b field swap)" both fail — the
  swap is not a field swap, it is a different table.
- at `d_B`: same, mirrored.

**Repair.** Choose a symmetric placement and write it down. Two work: (i) split
the mass equally at `d_A` and `d_B`; (ii) spread it uniformly over
`[min(d_A,d_B), max(d_A,d_B)]`, which is symmetric, keeps the total, and is more
honest — the disagreement genuinely occupies that span, and it makes the
`λ_date` term visible in the timeline rather than teleporting it. Either keeps
the table closing. Also state that the `κ` event constant carries units of
quarters, which is what makes an atom's contribution commensurable with
JND·quarters of sustained deviation.

## M18 — MINOR. P-C8's "exactly 0" is not exactly 0 in IEEE754

**Claim under attack.** P-C8: "an explicit neutral instruction (rubato intensity
1, lateStart 0, earlyEnd 1; asynchrony 0ms) ≡ absent map: dimension distance
**exactly 0**". P-C1 makes the same "exactly 0" promise.

With `intensity = 1`, `lateStart = 0`, `earlyEnd = 1`, §5.2's displacement is
`δ = frameLength·((τ/frameLength)^1·(1−0) + 0) − τ = frameLength·(τ/frameLength) −
τ`. `Math.pow(x, 1) === x` holds (verified over 10⁶ random doubles), but
`fl·(τ/fl) − τ` does not round-trip for all integer pairs. Scanning 71 630
integer `(frameLength, τ)` pairs found violations, e.g.

    frameLength = 22, τ = 15  →  δ = −1.7763568394002505e−15
    frameLength = 25, τ =  7  →  δ = +8.881784197001252e−16

so `d_rubato` against the absent map is a small positive number, not 0, and a test
asserting exact equality fails on a fixture that happens to pick such a pair.

**Repair.** Either weaken the assertion to `≤ ε·L` (and say so in R2/P-C1/P-C8),
or — better, since it is free — special-case the neutral warp: when
`intensity === 1 && lateStart === 0 && earlyEnd === 1`, return `δ ≡ 0` without
arithmetic. The same guard should be applied wherever "exactly 0" is promised for
an algebraically-neutral parametrization.

## M19 — MINOR. Degenerate-corpus guards missing where `null` is promised

**Claims under attack.** §8: "`normalization: 'corpus'`: one constant ω vector
derived from the matrix (**per-dimension median of nonzero `d_k`**), stamped";
"silhouette per k as guidance"; §9: "every numeric finite or null".

- A dimension can be identically zero across a whole corpus — a corpus of piano
  rolls with no `rubatoMap` anywhere makes every `d_rubato = 0`. The median of a

- Silhouette `s(i) = (b−a)/max(a,b)` is `0/0` when a corpus contains only

- The design should also say whether `ω_k = 1/median_k` or `median_k`; §8 says
  only "derived", which is not enough to reproduce a stamped constant.

**Repair.** Specify: an all-zero dimension keeps `ω_k = 1` (any positive constant
is metric-safe; 1 is the documented default) and is flagged in
`normalizationConstants`; `max(a,b) = 0 ⟹ s(i) = 0` by the same convention
already adopted for singleton clusters; and write the formula for the corpus
constant explicitly.

## Verified — claims I attacked and could not break

Recorded so the conductor knows the negative space of this review.

- **The ρ-table constants are correct.** Re-derived independently:
  uniform↔Gaussian `ρ = 2∫₀¹(u−½)Φ⁻¹(u)du·√3 = √3/√π = √(3/π) = 0.977205023806`;
  uniform↔symmetric-triangular `ρ = √18·(7/30) = 7√2/10 = 0.989949493661`
  (by direct integration of the triangular quantile `−1+√(2u)` against
  `√3(2u−1)`). Both match §5.9 to 12 digits.
- **Ruzzo–Tompa canonicity survives exact zeros.** A segment extended by a
  zero-score cell contains a proper subsequence of *equal* score and therefore
  fails RT's maximality condition, so boundary zeros are never absorbed, interior
  zeros are (correctly) kept, and the maximal set stays unique. §7.3's "no tie
  rules needed" is right about the set. (It is wrong about the *ranking* — M9d.)
- **The §5.4 MSM-less accentuation approximation is metric-safe**, which is not
  obvious: comparing two patterns over `lcm(len_A, len_B)` looks pair-dependent,
  but `|X − Y|` has period `lcm(len_A, len_B)`, so its mean over that period
  equals its mean over `lcm(len_A, len_B, len_C)`. All three pairwise means are
  therefore means over one common period and the triangle inequality holds
  pointwise.
- **Summing pseudo-metrics with per-dimension invariance modes is safe.** Mixing
  `'none'`/`'level'`/`'level-gain'` across dimensions preserves the axioms,
  because each `d_k` is separately a pseudo-metric and Proposition 4 needs only
  constant non-negative `ω`.
- **The unpinned event aligner is a pseudo-metric** (the composition argument
  survives a `λ_date` term because the DP's `min` always leaves delete+insert
  available). The defect in M5 is the pre-pinning, not the DP.
- **Atoms in the measure do not threaten the exact decomposition.** `p_k` as a
  measure with an absolutely continuous part plus atoms is still countably
  additive, so R4's closure argument holds verbatim; the `κ` constants are a
  positive per-dimension scalar and preserve every axiom.
- **Bit-exact symmetry is achievable on the semantic path** (sorted-union grid,
  `|x−y|`, date-ordered Neumaier, per-document Bézier bisection), subject to the
  sign-comparison proviso noted in M16.
