### [MAJOR] Gesture-scope end-marker duplicate is detected in quarter-note space but written in the duplicate's own beat unit

- **Claim:** `markEndMarkerDuplicates` matches the duplicate in NORMALIZED space (`target.reading.value * instruction.normalization !== next.level.reading.value * next.normalization`, levels.ts:763-764), but `moveEndMarkerDuplicate` writes `target.after` — already denormalized by the *transition's* `@beatLength` — straight into the duplicate's `@bpm` (levels.ts:788) without re-normalizing for the duplicate's own `@beatLength`. When the two instructions carry different `@beatLength`, the mechanism whose stated purpose is "one musical value at two sites" writes two different musical values, and introduces the very discontinuity it exists to prevent.
- **Evidence:** src/expression/levels.ts:763-764 (normalized detection) vs levels.ts:788 (un-normalized write).

Input:
```xml
<tempoMap>
  <tempo id="t1" date="0.0" bpm="120" beatLength="0.25" transition.to="60"/>
  <tempo id="t2" date="4.0" bpm="30"  beatLength="0.5"/>
</tempoMap>
```
`applyExaggeration(root, {tempo: 2}, {scope: 'gesture'})`. Both sites denote 60 quarter-note bpm (60·1 and 30·2), which is why the duplicate is detected at all.
Hand arithmetic: pair in quarter-note space (240·0.5? no — 120·1 and 60·1) gives μ = √(120·60) = 84.8528137423857; to' = μ(60/μ)² = 42.426406871192846 quarter-bpm. The duplicate must therefore receive 42.426406871192846 / 2 = 21.213203435596423 half-note bpm.
Actual: t1 → bpm=169.70562748477144, transition.to=42.42640687119286; t2 → bpm="42.42640687119286", which is 84.85281374238572 quarter-bpm — exactly 2× the value it is supposed to share, and moving UP from 60 while its partner moved DOWN to 42.43. Report note: "moved with its transition endpoint to 42.42640687119286: the MEI export writes one musical value at two sites, and they are not independent levers".
